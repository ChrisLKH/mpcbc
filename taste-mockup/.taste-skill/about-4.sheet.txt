This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts
with a stated brand constraint or needs an asset that does not exist, and say why in your report.

The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
One short sentence, set at poster scale, is the composition. The trick that makes it an idea rather than a big headline: something from the subject sits inside the sentence where a word would be. A strip of photograph fills the gap between two words; a small live fragment of the product replaces the noun; the object the sentence names is pictured at the size of a letter. Write the sentence first, find the word that can be shown instead of said, and put the showing in its place. The reader completes the sentence with their eyes.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
The sentence is four to seven words and runs as one or two full-width lines at 9–13vw. Inline with the type, on the baseline, sits one media slot: a rounded-rectangle or square frame exactly one line-height tall and two to three ems wide, holding a photograph or a looping crop. Above and below the sentence the screen is empty except for a small navigation line and one supporting sentence set small, both on the same left margin. Later sections repeat the grammar at smaller scale: each is a short statement on the left six columns with its proof (image, figure, list) on the right, so the page reads as a series of sentences with evidence. Generous vertical space (160px or more) between sections.

## 3. Type
Kept from the brand. Display weight with real presence (medium to bold), tracking -0.03 to -0.05em, leading 0.95–1.0 so two lines sit close. The inline frame aligns to cap height and baseline exactly; set it with em units so it scales with the type.

## 4. Colour
Kept from the brand. A calm ground with ink at full contrast. The only colour comes from the photograph inside the sentence; if the palette has an accent, use it once, on the action.

## 5. Imagery
**Use the images that belong to the project in every picture slot.** For slots they cannot fill, real pictures of the subject's world from the open collections come next (start with `node C:/Projects/mpcbc/.agents/skills/design-taste-frontend/scripts/assets.mjs "alhambra postcard"`, then "alhambra old map" and "church", then queries from your inventory (its street, its materials, its trade's tools and history); credit them on the page), then the dealt photographs.

One strong, simple photograph that reads at small size (a face, an object, a texture, a horizon), cropped to a wide strip inside the sentence. The same image may open to full width in the next section, so the small slot feels like a window onto something larger.

**Floor (the inspector checks it):** at least four real pictures on the page, usually six to twelve; SVG drawings do not count. One moving picture where an honest clip exists: `node <skill>/scripts/assets.mjs --video "<query>"`, used as a muted loop with a poster frame. Whatever this direction says about pictures, it is carried out with real ones.

## 6. Hero — Sentence split by a frame
A white or pale ground. One short sentence is broken in two and pushed to the edges: the first half flush to the 32px left margin, the second half flush to the right margin, both on the vertical centre line at clamp(40px, 3.6vw, 60px), medium weight. Between them, dead centre on columns 5–8, sits one media frame: 35% of the viewport wide, 16:9, corners 4px, holding a photograph or a working fragment of the product. The frame belongs to a vertical reel: the bottom 60px of the previous frame shows at the top edge of the viewport and the top 60px of the next at the bottom edge, same width, 24px gaps, so the centre column reads as a strip passing through the sentence. The stage is sticky; the reel advances three or four steps while the two halves of the sentence hold their place. Wordmark top-left; three text links and the single action as a small solid button top-right, all at 15px. Bottom-left on columns 1–4: one supporting sentence at 18px, its last line 32px above the bottom edge. The other corners stay empty.

On a phone: The halves stack: the first half of the sentence at 9vw flush left under the wordmark, then the frame at full width minus 16px margins and 4:3, then the second half flush right at 9vw. The neighbouring frames show as 28px slivers above and below. The supporting sentence and the action button follow at 16px. The links collapse to one word.
Headline: One sentence of four to six words split into two halves of up to about 14 characters each; the frame between them shows what it is about.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names colours (white): map them onto this palette's roles (ground `--bg`, panels `--surface` and the fields, text `--ink`/`--muted`, the accent `--accent` on the primary action only).
- It sets a sentence large over several lines: jurors read that as a long headline. Either cut it to a two-line headline at display size, or keep it a statement at 36px or less on three lines (24px, four lines on a phone).

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. intro — Headline crossing onto a photograph (split)
A white or pale ground. A photograph taller than it is wide, 720px wide (columns 7–12), sits flush against the right viewport edge with its top at 46% of the section's first screen and runs below the fold. The heading starts on the 32px left margin across columns 1–8: two lines of light-weight serif capitals at clamp(64px, 5.6vw, 88px), leading 1.04, the first line starting 34% down the screen and the second placed so that its right half runs onto the photograph's upper-left corner. Choose a picture whose upper-left quarter is pale enough for the ink, or set the second line twice, in the ink and in the paper colour, each copy clipped to its side of the photograph's left edge. Under the heading on columns 1–5, one paragraph of up to 25 words at 22px, leading 1.4. At the bottom-left a second, smaller photograph of 360×400px enters, cropped by the screen's lower edge, and on the next screen becomes the left half of a staggered pair with the large one. Nothing else is on the screen.
On a phone: The heading sits at 10vw on two lines on the 20px margin. The photograph becomes 75% of the width, flush right, and starts under the heading's first line so the second line crosses its top-left corner. The paragraph follows at 17px, then the small photograph at 50% of the width on the left margin.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names a kind of typeface: use the brand faces.
- It names colours (white): map them onto this palette's roles (ground `--bg`, panels `--surface` and the fields, text `--ink`/`--muted`, the accent `--accent` on the primary action only).
- It numbers things 01, 02: drop the numbers unless they are real data (a price, a date, a count).

### 2. about — Side facts, wide paragraph (table)
Two rows share one vertical line at 34% of the viewport width. Row one: in a 160px column at the left margin, three label and value pairs stack with 20px between them, each a label in the soft ink with its value beneath in full ink, all at 13px (founded, based in, team size). From the 34% line a five-line paragraph at clamp(20px, 1.7vw, 26px), leading 1.35, runs to 90% of the width. Row two starts 120px lower and repeats the logic for the record of the practice: one plain word at 13px at the far left (awards, press, clients), then from the 34% line two list columns, each 260px wide with a 64px gutter, at 13px with 10px between lines: name at the left, year at the right in the soft ink. No heading; the paragraph opens the section. Each list comfortably holds 6–12 lines. No photographs are needed.
On a phone: Everything aligns to the left margin. The paragraph comes first at 19px. The three label and value pairs follow as a two-column grid at 14px. The two record lists stack with 40px between them, each opened by its plain word in full ink.

### 3. list — Line-up at one type size (table)
A full-bleed flat section. The programme is a four-column table spanning the full width between 24px margins, with every cell at one size, clamp(20px, 2.1vw, 30px), one weight, no bold: the act or session flush left, the format starting at 33%, the room or stage at 66%, and the time range right-aligned at the far edge with tabular figures. Rows are 1.5em tall and separated by nothing but that leading; the fixed column starts do the organising. Day or part headings are a single line at the same size in the accent ink with 72px above and 20px below, and may be `position: sticky; top: 0` on a strip of the section's ground colour so the current day stays in view. Breaks and changeovers are rows set in capitals in the soft ink. There is no other heading. Comfortably holds 12–40 rows; with fewer than eight, raise the size to 3vw.
On a phone: Each entry becomes two lines: the act at 22px on the first, then format, room and time on one line at 14px in the soft ink with the time right-aligned. 18px between entries. Day headings stay sticky at the top on the ground colour.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names a kind of typeface: use the brand faces.

### 4. features — Statements on colour fields (statement)
Three or four full-bleed flat colour fields, each 72–80svh tall, butted together with no gap. Each field holds one benefit and is almost empty. The benefit is a phrase of two to four words at clamp(56px, 9vw, 150px), heavy, leading 0.9, on two lines at most, hugging the bottom-left corner with 28px margins. Its explanation is three lines at 17–18px, 30ch wide, placed near the top at column 8, so the eye travels the diagonal from small text to big phrase. On alternate fields the arrangement mirrors: phrase bottom-right and right-aligned, paragraph top-left at column 2. Text on each field is ink or paper, whichever contrasts more. There is no separate section heading: the first field's phrase is the heading. Nothing else appears on the fields: no images, shapes or buttons.
On a phone: Fields are 64svh tall. The paragraph sits top-left at 16px across 80% of the width; the phrase sits bottom-left at 13vw on two lines. Mirroring is dropped; alternate fields right-align the paragraph only, keeping the phrase on the left margin.

### 5. cta — The idea again, inverted (statement)
The close flips the page's colours: ink becomes the ground and the ground becomes the ink, across a full-bleed field at least 90svh tall. The page's opening idea returns as a headline of three to five words at 9–11vw on two lines, leading 0.9, flush left, positioned in the upper two-thirds; it should echo the hero's wording, not repeat it. Along the bottom, on one shared baseline 40px above the edge: one supporting sentence at 20px in columns 1–5 and one rectangular button in the accent colour, right-aligned in columns 10–12, about 56px tall. Nothing else: no image, no second button, no logo. The field's top edge is a hard horizontal line where it meets the section above. If the footer follows on the same inverted field, leave 160px before it so the close still reads as its own screen.
On a phone: The same inverted field at 80svh. Headline at 15vw on two lines at the top. The sentence sits lower at 17px, and the button spans the full width at the bottom, 56px tall, with 20px margins. Nothing else is added.

### 6. contact — The email as display type (statement)
The section is 100svh in the inverse of the page's colours. A single email address is set as one line of display type spanning the content width: the font-size is fitted so the address runs from margin to margin (about 7–9vw for 20 characters; break after the @ onto two lines if it is longer than 26), light or regular weight, tight tracking, sitting on a baseline 12vh above the bottom edge. It is a `mailto:` link; on hover the whole line takes the accent ink. In the top-left corner, a sentence of up to 12 words at 20–22px says what to write about. In the two bottom corners at 14px: phone and city at the left, two or three social links at the right. No heading and no form. For a pinned ending, make the section `position: sticky; bottom: 0` beneath the page so the previous section lifts off it.
On a phone: The address breaks after the @ onto two lines at 11.5vw, left-aligned, sitting at 55% of an 86svh screen. The sentence sits at the top-left at 18px. Phone, city and social links stack at the bottom-left at 15px with 12px between them.

### 7. footer — Contact as the headline (statement)
A full-bleed field in one saturated palette colour, at least 90svh tall. Top row, 40px from the edge: two or three short notes at 15px in columns 1–7 (address, hours, a one-line description) and a back-to-top text link at the right margin. Then a tall empty gap. In the lower half, the way to get in touch is the display line: the telephone number or the email address itself, set at 8–9vw, flush left, hard against the margin, as one link. An email address may break once, before the @, giving two lines. Size it with `clamp()` so the longest line fills at least 80% of the width. Under it, one row of small text at 14px spread across three positions: links at the left, credits in the centre, city and year at the right. The wordmark appears only small, in the top row.
On a phone: Notes stack at the top at 15px. The contact line sits in the lower half at 12vw; a telephone number stays on one line, an email breaks before the @ and is sized so each half fits the width. The meta row becomes three stacked lines at 13px.

## 8. Motion — Pinned story
One element holds its place with `position: sticky` (a title, a frame, a product fragment) while the items that belong to it scroll past or swap inside it. The page's ordinary scrolling does the work; nothing is hijacked. Hold it for about one screen per step and three screens at most, two held stages per page at most, with a line of text on every held screen. Recipe: 'Pinned story' in references/motion.md.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
The collision of big type and one live image inside it. Because there is only one image on the first screen, choose it with care and give it perfect alignment to the letters.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
The sentence must be short enough for two lines on a phone, where the inline frame drops to its own line between the two halves. Do not style one word differently to fake the device. Do not use more than one slot per sentence.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. The frame: one h1 on the first screen that says what this is, equal outer margins, no
section huddled on one side with the other half empty, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.

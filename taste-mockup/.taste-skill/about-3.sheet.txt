The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
For a place, the photograph is the argument. One full-bleed image of where you would be standing fills the first screen, and the design steps back: the name sits along the bottom edge like a film title, or small in a corner, and nothing else interrupts the view. The page that follows keeps that restraint: few words, large pictures, long pauses. The designer's work here is editing: choosing the one frame that makes someone want to be there, and then not covering it.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
First screen: a single photograph at 100vw by 100svh, focal point kept clear of text. The name is set either very large (14–20vw) hugging the bottom edge with 16–24px of margin, or small (20–28px) top-left with a one-line description bottom-left. Navigation is two to four words in the top corners in the same small size. If text sits on the image, a soft neutral scrim under the text area only. Below, sections alternate between a short centred or left-set paragraph in a narrow column (max 52ch) with wide empty margins, and photographs shown large: one full-bleed, then a pair at different sizes, then one portrait offset to the right. Practical information (hours, address, rates, booking) is gathered once near the end in a plain two-column block.

## 3. Type
Kept from the brand. A face with some grace for the name; small, calm, well-spaced text everywhere else. Sentence case. Body 17–19px with leading around 1.55.

## 4. Colour
Kept from the brand. Neutral paper or deep neutral ground chosen to flatter the photographs; text ink drawn from the darkest tone in the imagery. Colour lives in the pictures.

## 5. Imagery
**Use the images that belong to the project in every picture slot.** For slots they cannot fill, real pictures of the subject's world from the open collections come next (start with `node C:/Projects/mpcbc/.agents/skills/design-taste-frontend/scripts/assets.mjs "alhambra postcard"`, then "alhambra old map" and "church", then queries from your inventory (its street, its materials, its trade's tools and history); credit them on the page), then the dealt photographs.

The dealt set is the palette. Choose four to six photographs with the same light. The hero image must have a calm area for the name. Full-bleed or large with square corners; never thumbnails, never a carousel of ten.

**Floor (the inspector checks it):** at least four real pictures on the page, usually six to twelve; SVG drawings do not count. One moving picture where an honest clip exists: `node <skill>/scripts/assets.mjs --video "<query>"`, used as a muted loop with a poster frame. Whatever this direction says about pictures, it is carried out with real ones.

Photographs dealt for this build, from the "People" set (figures, silhouettes and portraits, mostly unposed)

## 6. Hero — Film title, bottom edge
One photograph fills the viewport at 100vw by 100svh, `object-fit: cover`, with its subject in the upper two-thirds and a calmer, darker zone along the bottom. The name is one condensed uppercase word, or a short name in a high-contrast serif, set at clamp(150px, 17.5vw, 290px) and sized to span the full width between 32px margins. It is centred and anchored so its baseline sits 34px above the bottom edge, covering roughly the lowest quarter of the frame. A soft neutral dark scrim lies under that bottom band only, about 40% of the height; the rest of the image is untouched. Directly above the name's left end, on the same margin, sits the supporting sentence at 16px on two short lines. Directly above its right end, right-aligned, sits the single action as a small paper-coloured rectangular button. The top edge carries a small emblem or wordmark at the left margin and up to five text links at 15px grouped at the right, all in white. The central half of the screen belongs to the view.

On a phone: The photograph is re-cropped to portrait with `object-position` on its subject and fills 100svh. The name stays on the bottom edge at 22vw on one line, or two lines at 20vw beyond eight characters, 20px above the edge. The supporting sentence sits above the name's left end at 15px. The action button moves to the top-right corner and the links collapse to a menu word beside the emblem.
Headline: The name of the place: one word of up to about 12 characters at 17.5vw, or two words stacked on a phone.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names a kind of typeface: use the brand faces.
- It names colours (white): map them onto this palette's roles (ground `--bg`, panels `--surface` and the fields, text `--ink`/`--muted`, the accent `--accent` on the primary action only).

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. intro — Heading captions a panorama (full-bleed)
The section is exactly 100svh. Its top third holds a two-line heading of three to six words at clamp(48px, 6.5vw, 96px) with leading 0.95, either centred or flush left on the page margin. 28px beneath the heading sit two small captions side by side, each two lines at 13px and 150px wide with a 48px gap, aligned to the heading (centred as a pair, or both starting at its left edge). 40px below them a full-bleed photograph begins and runs off the bottom of the viewport: it is 100vw wide at 21:9 or wider, and only its top 55–60svh is visible before the fold, the rest arriving with scroll. The heading therefore reads as a large caption to the picture. On an object-led page the image is the object on a flat dark panel inset by the page margin instead of bleeding to the edges.
On a phone: Heading at 12vw on two lines, left-aligned, taking the top 30% of the screen. The two captions merge into one four-line block at 14px. The photograph begins at 45% of the screen height in a 4:5 crop, full-bleed, and runs off the bottom.

### 2. about — Wide photo, colour tile, facts (mosaic)
Top: a heading of two to four words at clamp(40px, 4vw, 60px) on the left margin, then 24px below it a three-line lead paragraph at 21–22px across columns 1–6. 72px lower, two tiles sit side by side with a 16px gap. The left tile spans columns 1–8: a landscape photograph at 16:10 with one radius (0 or 10px). A short paragraph of up to 35 words at 16px is set in a solid paper-colour block 320px wide with 20px padding, placed over the photograph's top-right corner and flush with both edges. The right tile spans columns 9–12 and is a flat palette colour, 60% of the photograph's height: it holds a simple inline SVG outline of the region or the floor plan as one filled shape, with one solid dot marking the location. Under the colour tile, four label and value pairs at 14px in a two-by-two grid: altitude, distance, season, local time.
On a phone: Heading at 9vw, lead at 18px. The photograph goes full width at 4:3 and its overlaid paragraph moves beneath it as ordinary text. The colour tile follows at full width and 3:2, and the four label and value pairs sit under it in two columns.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets text at 10px: use your small step (13px or more) instead.

### 3. list — Two-ended rows, narrow column (list)
A list in one narrow column, 520px wide, starting at 22% of the viewport width or centred. Every row is 34–38px tall with no box and nothing drawn between rows. Each row has two ends: at the left, a 16px round image (a face, a logo, a colour dot) followed after 8px by the name at 15–16px in full ink; at the far right of the column, a short value at 13px in the soft ink (a date, a price, a domain, an opening time) with tabular figures. Entries that need a sentence run to two lines at the left, and the value aligns with the first line. Groups are separated by 40px of space and begin with one plain word in the soft ink at the same size. A heading of two to four words at 24–28px in medium weight sits 32px above the first row. Holds 10–40 rows.
On a phone: The column fills the width with 20px margins. Rows are 44px tall for touch; the left image stays 16px. Long values wrap beneath the name at 13px, left-aligned with it, and the row grows. Group spacing is 32px.

### 4. features — Rows with margin photographs (list)
Everything hangs from one vertical axis at column 5, about 35% of the width. First the heading: a statement of up to five words at clamp(40px, 5vw, 80px) starting on the axis, vertically centred in an otherwise empty 70svh of ground. Then four or five benefit rows, each starting on the same axis: a title at clamp(28px, 3vw, 48px) and under it a paragraph of 34ch at 17px. Columns 1–4 stay empty. At the right margin each row carries one photograph, top-aligned with its title and deliberately unequal: 240px wide at 4:5 in one row, 320px at 3:2 in the next, 180px square after that. Rows are separated by 96–120px of space, not lines. On a saturated field, set all type in one pale ink and leave the photographs ungraded.
On a phone: The axis collapses to the left margin. The statement sits at 9vw in 50svh of empty ground. Each row stacks its title at 26px, the paragraph, then its photograph at alternating widths: 100%, 70% flush right, 55% flush left. 64px between rows.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets a sentence large over several lines: jurors read that as a long headline. Either cut it to a two-line headline at display size, or keep it a statement at 36px or less on three lines (24px, four lines on a phone).

### 5. cta — Last photograph, text beside (full-bleed)
A final full-bleed photograph, 100vw by 90svh, chosen so its subject sits in the left half and its right half is calm. All text is confined to the right half and never crosses the centre line: in columns 8–12, anchored to the lower third, a closing line of three to five words at about 3.4vw in a light weight, a paragraph of two or three lines at 17px, and one filled button in the paper colour with ink text, about 52px tall. If the image behind the text is not calm enough, darken the whole photograph slightly with `filter: brightness(0.8)` rather than laying a panel behind the words. The section butts directly against a footer in one solid colour, so the photograph's bottom edge is a hard line. No logo, no second action.
On a phone: The photograph is 100vw by 70svh, cropped to keep its subject. The text leaves the image and sits beneath it on the footer's solid colour: closing line at 28px, paragraph at 17px, full-width button. Image and colour meet at a hard edge.

### 6. contact — Address in display rows (list)
The contact block is a stack of five full-width rows, every one set at the same size, clamp(44px, 6vw, 88px), regular weight, left-aligned with a 20px inset and leading 1.0: the building or venue name, the street, the city with postcode, the opening hours as one compact line, the phone number. Each row is about 150px tall with the text sitting in its lower third; rows are told apart by alternating two flat tones of the section colour as full-width bands, or by 28px of space alone. A sixth row holds a one-word greeting at the same size followed, 24px to its right and centred on the x-height, by a solid round button 0.9em across with an arrow, which opens the email or booking link. Street and phone rows are links (map, call). No heading and no form. Optionally, one small round photograph sits at the right end of the first row.
On a phone: Rows at 9.6vw with 16px insets, about 84px tall; the hours break onto two rows (days, then times). The greeting row becomes a full-width band in the accent colour with the round arrow button at its right end, 56px across.

### 7. footer — Contact as the headline (statement)
A full-bleed field in one saturated palette colour, at least 90svh tall. Top row, 40px from the edge: two or three short notes at 15px in columns 1–7 (address, hours, a one-line description) and a back-to-top text link at the right margin. Then a tall empty gap. In the lower half, the way to get in touch is the display line: the telephone number or the email address itself, set at 8–9vw, flush left, hard against the margin, as one link. An email address may break once, before the @, giving two lines. Size it with `clamp()` so the longest line fills at least 80% of the width. Under it, one row of small text at 14px spread across three positions: links at the left, credits in the centre, city and year at the right. The wordmark appears only small, in the top row.
On a phone: Notes stack at the top at 15px. The contact line sits in the lower half at 12vw; a telephone number stays on one line, an email breaks before the @ and is sized so each half fits the width. The meta row becomes three stacked lines at 13px.

## 8. Motion — Slow crossfade
The principal photograph quietly changes: three or four images of the same framing crossfade over about 1.6 seconds, holding five to six seconds each, or the fixed image changes when its matching section arrives. It should feel like light shifting in a room. Pause when the tab is hidden; under reduced motion show only the first image.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
The photographs, at full size, uninterrupted. Empty paper around short text makes the next image land harder.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
A weak hero photograph cannot be fixed by typography; pick again. Do not stack a headline, a paragraph and two buttons on the image. Do not use a booking bar that covers the bottom of the view; one small action is enough.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. The frame: one h1 on the first screen that says what this is, equal outer margins, no
section huddled on one side with the other half empty, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
